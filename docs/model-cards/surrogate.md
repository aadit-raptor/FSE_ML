## How it is tested

- **Split:** No time split: trained on simulated deals, which have no dates; the cases are the sourced figures recorded on 2026-10-06.
- **Baseline:** The simulation itself on 200 paths, fast enough to run live; truth is the simulation on 10,000 paths.
- **Headline:** `median_irr_error_pp` on the `all_regional_deals` set. A group "beats the baseline" only when the model is strictly better there; under 5 cases it has "not enough data" and the app should show no estimate.
- **Regions:** S&P's (the validation report's): United States, Europe, emerging markets, other developed.

Statistics:

- `median_irr_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the median IRR, percentage points.
- `p5_irr_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the 5th-percentile IRR, percentage points.
- `p95_irr_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the 95th-percentile IRR, percentage points.
- `wipeout_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the share of paths that lose all equity, percentage points.

## Limitations

- Every term outside the eleven inputs is the fixed training deal's; the Live screen lists the deal's differences. PLAN.md 5.8 retrains across deal terms.
- Sourced regional inputs often fall outside the training ranges (D&A, working capital and capex shares below them, exit multiples and their spreads above them); the network extrapolates there, and the in-training-range set shows how it does inside them.
- Truth is itself a simulation of 10,000 paths, so a median error below about 0.1 points is within its noise.
