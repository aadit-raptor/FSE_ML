## How it is tested

- **Split:** No time split: the cases are simulated deals, which have no dates: the sourced figures recorded on 2026-10-06.
- **Baseline:** The simulation itself on 200 paths, the cruder estimate a live answer could be; truth is the simulation on 100,000 paths.
- **Headline:** `median_irr_error_pp` on the `varied_deals` set. A group "beats the baseline" only when the model is strictly better there; under 5 cases it has "not enough data" and the app should show no estimate.
- **Regions:** S&P's (the validation report's): United States, Europe, emerging markets, other developed.

Statistics:

- `median_irr_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the median IRR, percentage points.
- `p5_irr_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the 5th-percentile IRR, percentage points.
- `p95_irr_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the 95th-percentile IRR, percentage points.
- `wipeout_error_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Mean absolute error of the share of paths that lose all equity, percentage points.

## Limitations

- The estimate carries sampling error: the Live step shows a 95% interval for the median, about half a percentage point wide for an ordinary deal and wider on fewer paths.
- Close to losing everything the 5th percentile falls steeply, so it moves more between samples than the median does (the p5 column).
- Truth is itself a simulation of 100,000 paths: a median error below about 0.04 points is within its noise.
- Whatever the simulation leaves out the estimate leaves out too (CLAUDE.md findings 12 and 13: the percentages path drops surplus cash once debt is repaid, and neither path reads minimum cash).
