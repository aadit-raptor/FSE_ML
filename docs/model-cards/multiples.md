## How it is tested

- **Split:** Walk-forward by the year of the multiple predicted: trained on cases before each cutoff (2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025), tested on the cases from it to the next.
- **Baseline:** The region's whole market: the same percentiles (10th, 50th, 90th) across every industry of the group in the base year, whatever the deal's industry.
- **Headline:** `interval_score` on the `entry` set. A group "beats the baseline" only when the model is strictly better there; under 5 cases it has "not enough data" and the app should show no estimate.
- **Regions:** S&P's (the validation report's): United States, Europe, emerging markets, other developed.

Statistics:

- `interval_score` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.05): The central 80% range's width plus ten times how far the multiple fell outside it, in turns of EBITDA (Gneiting and Raftery's interval score).
- `inside` (higher is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.01): Share of multiples inside the central 80% range (about 0.8 when the range is right).
- `median_error` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.05): Mean distance between the suggestion (the range's middle) and the multiple, in turns.
- `width` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.05): Mean width of the range, in turns.

## Limitations

- Cases are industries' aggregate multiples, not single companies or buyouts: a buyout's price also reflects its size, growth and control, which the averages don't split out.
- The archive starts with 2011 and some groups miss a few editions (Australia, New Zealand and Canada miss 2011, 2013 and 2014; China 2014 and 2023), so the longest exit horizons are tested on the newest years only.
- A horizon needs 30 earlier industry moves in the group; the early folds of the longest horizons had fewer and are left out.
- Cases from the same year share that year's market: the regions' statistics move together and count fewer independent years than cases.
- Out of time the ranges held about three multiples in four, where four in five is the aim: industries' multiples moved more after each cutoff than before it, so read the range as a little narrow.
- The averages are listed companies' and are not split by size.
