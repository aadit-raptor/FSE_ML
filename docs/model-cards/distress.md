## How it is tested

- **Split:** No time split: the predictor fits nothing to outcomes, so no case can leak into it; every case reads the tables of S&P's 2024 study and its January 2024 methodology.
- **Baseline:** The deal summary's default risk: year-one EBIT / interest through Damodaran's coverage table, and S&P's global cumulative default rate for that rating over the hold.
- **Headline:** `auc` on the `reference_deals` set. A group "beats the baseline" only when the model is strictly better there; under 5 cases it has "not enough data" and the app should show no estimate.
- **Regions:** S&P's (the validation report's): United States, Europe, emerging markets, other developed.

Statistics:

- `auc` (higher is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.005): Chance a distressed deal is given a higher chance of default than a deal that wasn't (0.5 = no better than chance).
- `brier` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.005): Mean squared gap between the chance of default over the hold and what happened (distress = 1).
- `mean_abs_gap_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.005): Mean gap (percentage points) between the implied cumulative default rate and S&P's for the same band, region and horizon.
- `max_abs_gap_pp` (lower is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.005): The largest of those gaps.

## Limitations

- Ten sourced transactions: only the United States has enough (six, two distressed); every other region shows not enough data.
- On those ten deals it does not rank distress better than the year-one default risk: Dollar General (10x leverage) and Avago (13x) exited well while Toys "R" Us and Gymboree (about 6.5x) failed years later. The app therefore shows the bands but not the probabilities anywhere today.
- Truth is distress at any time after closing; the predictor's horizon is the hold, so a failure years after an exit counts against it.
- S&P's regional tables print letter grades only and ten years for Europe and emerging markets; past the last year the last year's rate is held.
- The business risk profile is the user's choice (4, fair, unless changed); where Table 3 gives two anchors the weaker is read.
- Calibration is exact by construction for a deal that stays in one band; a deal that moves between bands reads each band's rate at its age, which S&P's tables don't test.
