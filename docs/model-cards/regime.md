## How it is tested

- **Split:** Walk-forward by the year named; its cut-offs come from the region's earlier years and its figure from the IMF's report of that year: trained on cases before each cutoff (2000, 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022), tested on the cases from it to the next.
- **Baseline:** The same regime as last year, from the same report's estimate of last year.
- **Headline:** `accuracy` on the `spring` set. A group "beats the baseline" only when the model is strictly better there; under 5 cases it has "not enough data" and the app should show no estimate.
- **Regions:** the seven the regime is given for: United States, euro area, United Kingdom, Japan, China, India and the world.

Statistics:

- `accuracy` (higher is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.02): Share of years whose regime was named as the year's final figures give it.
- `turn_accuracy` (higher is better; CI fails a change that makes it worse by more than 0.05): The same share over the years that turned out a recession, stagflation or bull year.

## Limitations

- The IMF's historical forecasts file ends with its 2022 reports, so later years are not tested until the IMF extends it.
- Earlier years' figures are today's, not those known at the time: revisions since then reach a tested year's cut-offs and its previous-five-year median.
- One regime a year from two figures: no quarterly turning points, no markets, no credit conditions. A downturn that starts late in a year shows the year after.
- A fifth of a region's years is a recession by construction: the word means weak against its own record, not two quarters of falling output.
- India's figures are for fiscal years starting in April, which the IMF files under the calendar year they start in.
- The euro area and the world are the IMF's own aggregates, as composed at each report; the euro area's start in 1992, so its first tested year is later than the others'.
- A deal outside the six economies and the euro area is shown the world's regime, which may differ from its own country's.
